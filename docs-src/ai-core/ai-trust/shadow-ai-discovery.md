# Shadow AI Discovery

You cannot govern AI you do not know exists. **Shadow AI** — much like shadow IT — refers to AI systems operating outside formal governance processes: employees using unauthorized AI applications, and increasingly, internal teams building agents, integrating foundation models, connecting MCP servers, and deploying autonomous workflows without governance visibility.

**AI Asset Discovery** in IBM watsonx.governance identifies both governed and ungoverned AI assets directly from supported agent development and orchestration platforms, bringing shadow AI into governed workflows.

## Why This Matters

- **AI is being built faster than governance can track it.** New AI systems emerge across business units and dev teams faster than use-case submissions, questionnaires, and self-attestations can keep up — 27% of organizations don't know where AI is in use.
- **An agent's risk profile is bigger than the agent.** It depends on connected tools, MCP servers, foundation models, and collaborator agents — a flat inventory misses these relationships.
- **AI systems evolve quickly.** Teams swap models, add tools, and change configurations; without continuous discovery, governance drifts out of sync with the real AI estate.

## Key Capabilities

| Capability | What It Does |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Automated asset discovery** | Continuously scan agent platforms (AWS Bedrock, Azure AI Foundry, watsonx Orchestrate) for governed and ungoverned agents, tools, MCP servers, and foundation models |
| **Relationship mapping** | Capture the full system around each agent — versions, deployment environments, connected tools, MCP servers, models, and collaborator agents |
| **Semantic matching & onboarding** | AI-enabled similarity matching links discovered assets to existing governance records or onboards them as new, auto-triggering risk, control, and evidence workflows |
| **Drift detection** | Automatically reflect configuration changes in watsonx.governance, with activity logs of what changed and when for an auditable record |
